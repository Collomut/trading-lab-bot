Essential Terms & Concept Guide

A plain-English breakdown of all trading metrics, risk formulas, and mathematical indicators used by your automated system.

1. Account & Money Metrics
Total Account Net Worth (Equity)
Balance
The true, current market value of your entire account right now. It equals your unallocated cash balance PLUS the live market value of any coins you currently hold.
Everyday Example: If you have $9,000 cash in your wallet and own a gold coin currently worth $1,050, your Net Worth is $10,050.
Unrealized P&L vs. Realized P&L
Performance
Unrealized P&L is the "paper" profit or loss on trades that are still open right now (fluctuates second by second).
Realized P&L is the permanent, banked cash profit or loss after a trade has been fully closed and sold.
Everyday Example: If you buy a house for $200k and someone offers you $220k, you have $20k in unrealized profit. Once you accept the offer and close the deed, that $20k becomes realized cash in your bank.
Accuracy Rate (Win Rate)
Metric
The percentage of completed trades that ended with a positive profit. Calculated as: (Winning Trades / Total Completed Trades) × 100.
Everyday Example: If the bot makes 10 trades, and 7 make a profit while 3 are stopped out, your accuracy rate is 70.0%.
2. Capital Protection & Safety Rules
Stop-Loss (SL)
Protection
An automatic order placed the millisecond you enter a trade to sell immediately if the price falls by 2%. It prevents small mistakes from turning into catastrophic losses.
Everyday Example: An emergency brake on a car. If the road ahead collapses, the car stops automatically before going off the cliff.
Take-Profit (TP)
Target
A preset exit price target (+3%) where the bot automatically sells to lock in cash gains. It prevents greed from giving back winning profits when the market reverses.
Everyday Example: Selling concert tickets the moment they hit the price you wanted, rather than holding on too long and seeing prices crash right before the show.
Break-Even Protection (Zero-Loss Shield)
Safety Mechanism
Once an open position climbs by +1.5% in profit, the bot automatically moves its stop-loss order up to the exact price it originally bought at. From that moment forward, it is impossible to lose money on that trade.
Everyday Example: In a casino game, taking your original $100 off the table once you're ahead, and playing only with the casino's money.
1% Risk Rule (Position Sizing)
Money Management
The bot sizes every trade so that if the worst-case scenario occurs and the stop-loss hits, you lose at most 1% of your account (e.g. max $100 on a $10,000 account).
Everyday Example: Never putting all your eggs in one basket. If one egg cracks, you still have 99 whole eggs.
Daily Circuit Breaker (Max Daily Loss)
Emergency Shield
If total losses in a single day reach 3% of your portfolio, the bot automatically halts all trading until the next calendar day. This prevents emotional revenge trading during violent market flash-crashes.
Everyday Example: An electrical fuse in your house. If too much current surges through the wires, the breaker trips to prevent a house fire.
3. Technical Analysis & Bot Brain
Timeframe (1h vs 15m)
Market Resolution
Each candlestick on the chart represents 1 hour of price movement. The 1-hour timeframe filters out erratic 5-minute wicks and false alarms, providing higher accuracy and cleaner momentum.
Everyday Example: Looking at climate trends instead of hourly temperature fluctuations.
Exponential Moving Average (EMA 9, 21, 50, 200)
Trend Filter
A line that calculates the average price over the last N candles, weighting recent prices more heavily. When short-term averages cross above long-term averages, it signals upward momentum. If price is below the 200 EMA, the bot refuses to buy.
Everyday Example: The slope of a boat in the water. If the bow is pointing down into a storm, you don't accelerate forward.
RSI (Relative Strength Index)
Momentum Oscillator
A number from 0 to 100 that measures whether a coin was dumped too fast (below 30 = "Oversold" / cheap bargain) or pumped too fast (above 70 = "Overbought" / expensive risk).
Everyday Example: A stretched rubber band. If it's stretched too far down, it is likely to snap back toward the center.
MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)
Momentum Shift
Shows the relationship between two moving averages of a price. When the MACD line crosses above its signal line, it confirms that buyers are taking control of the market.
Everyday Example: A speedometer accelerating. Even if a car is still moving slowly, seeing the speedometer shoot upward indicates speed is coming.
4. Execution Environment
Paper Trading (Testnet)
Simulation
Trading with simulated fake money on real live market price feeds. It allows you to test the bot's execution, discipline, and strategy in real market conditions without risking a single dollar.
Everyday Example: A flight simulator for pilots before flying a real Boeing 747.
Spot Trading (Non-Custodial API)
Security
Buying and selling the actual coin without borrowed money (no leverage, no liquidation risk). Because the bot uses Spot Trading API keys without withdrawal permissions, your funds cannot be transferred out of your account by anyone.
Everyday Example: Buying physical currency at an exchange counter. You own the bill outright without any debt.
