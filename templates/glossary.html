Plain language guide

Trading terms, explained

Short definitions for the words used in this dashboard. Examples are for learning only and do not predict a result.

First: risk is realPrices can move quickly, orders can fill at unexpected prices, and a stop monitored by an app can fail if the app is offline. No setting guarantees a maximum loss.

Money and account value

Cash balance

Money currently available in the exchange account for new orders. It is not the same as total account value if you also hold crypto.

Account value / net worth

Cash plus the estimated current value of tracked positions. It can be unavailable if the balance feed fails, and the app may not include every asset or liability in the exchange account.

Profit and loss (P&L)

A calculation of how much a position or group of trades gained or lost. This app’s trade log does not subtract exchange fees, slippage, or taxes. An open position’s P&L is an estimate that changes with price.

Realized and unrealized P&L

Realized means the app recorded an exit. Unrealized means an open position is being valued at the latest price; that value can change before an actual sale.

Win rate

The share of recorded closed trades whose P&L was positive. It ignores how large wins and losses were, so a high win rate can still lose money overall.

Orders and positions

Trading pair

The two assets in a market, such as BTC/USDT. BTC is the asset being bought or sold; USDT is the quoted price unit.

Position

The amount of an asset the bot believes it bought and has not yet recorded as sold. This dashboard does not independently reconcile that belief with the exchange account.

Market order and fill

A market order asks the exchange to buy or sell promptly at available prices. A fill is the quantity actually traded. The final average price and filled quantity can differ from the estimate shown before the order.

Stop-loss

A price level intended to trigger an exit after the market moves against a position. In this project the level is monitored by the app; it is not a stop order resting at the exchange. The app may be offline, and a market exit can fill below the level.

Take-profit

A price target intended to close a position after a favorable move. Here, the app monitors the target; it is not an exchange-side order, and a fill is not guaranteed.

Break-even and trailing stop

The bot can raise its monitored stop after the price has risen. “Break-even” ignores fees and execution costs, so an exit at the entry price can still lose money.

Risk and strategy

Risk per trade

A sizing estimate based on account value and the distance between entry and stop. It is a plan, not a maximum-loss guarantee: gaps, fees, and failures can make the actual loss larger.

Daily loss limit / circuit breaker

A configured threshold intended to stop new trades after losses. It does not automatically protect an already-open position. Correct operation also depends on persisted state and accurate account data.

Drawdown

How far account value has fallen from a previous high. A strategy can have many winning trades and still suffer a large drawdown.

Volatility

How much and how quickly prices tend to move. High volatility can make fills and stop exits less predictable.

Spread and liquidity

The spread is the gap between the best buy and sell prices. Liquidity describes how easily an order can trade without moving the price much. Thin liquidity can increase trading costs.

Risk and reward

A comparison between a possible loss and a possible gain. A target that is farther away than the stop does not make a strategy profitable by itself; the win and loss frequencies and costs also matter.

EMA, RSI, and MACD

EMA is a price average that gives more weight to recent prices. RSI summarizes recent upward versus downward moves. MACD compares price averages to describe momentum. They are calculations on past prices, not reliable forecasts.

Signal

A rule-based suggestion from the strategy to buy, sell, or wait. A signal is not a guarantee that the market will move in that direction.

Candlestick and timeframe

A candle summarizes opening, highest, lowest, and closing prices over a period. A 1-hour timeframe means each candle covers one hour; it does not mean the bot only checks once per hour.

Testing and reliability

Backtest

A simulation of rules on historical data. Results can be misleading if the test uses too little data, assumes unrealistic prices, omits costs, or is tuned to the same data it reports.

Paper trading / testnet

Practice orders using simulated funds or an exchange test environment. It helps check software flow but cannot fully reproduce live liquidity, fees, slippage, and order behavior.

Fees and slippage

Fees are exchange charges for trades. Slippage is the difference between the expected and actual execution price. Both reduce results; this project’s quick backtest does not model them.

Persistent storage

Storage that survives app redeploys. Railway’s files outside a mounted Volume are temporary. A volume helps retain files, but local files still do not guarantee exchange reconciliation or safe recovery.
